THE HUBParticipation Infrastructure for HPE's ERGs.

Platform

01Context

COMMUNITIES REAL. TOOLS IMPROVISED.

HPE's Employee Resource Groups were working, but through friction, not infrastructure. Membership over email. Events over calendar invites. Impact measured by hand.

HPE asked NCG to build The Hub: one platform for running ERGs, joining them, taking part and seeing their impact. I led UX across all modules, all roles, the web portal and the mobile PWA.

Two things from the early conversations with HPE shaped everything after. The way things worked wasn't engaging people. And the platform was meant to be about employee wellbeing, not something run by HR or marketing.

The Brief

“Streamline ERG operations, foster employee engagement, and support HPE’s commitment to diversity, equity, and inclusion.”

02The Real Problem

NOT FIVE PRODUCTS. ONE PLATFORM.

The usual way to build a platform with five roles is one design pass per role. It ships as the sum of its parts.

But The Hub is one place where five different people work on the same things, clubs, events, posts, members and feedback, from very different positions.

DESIGN THEM AS SEPARATE ROLE FLOWS AND YOU SHIP FIVE INTERACTION LANGUAGES FOR THE SAME SYSTEM.

Underneath was a second problem. This is a social space hosted by an employer. Making it feel voluntary and social, and not like something people attend because they have to, took decisions I had to argue for and hold through the project.

03Five Roles, One System

WHAT EACH ROLE IS TRYING TO KNOW.

Before any screen work, I mapped what each role is trying to know at any moment, not just their tasks.

What each role is trying to know
RoleWhat they want to know
Member“What's relevant to me, and how involved do I have to be?”
Club Owner“Is my club healthy, and what do I do to keep it growing?”
Event Manager“Is this event coming together, and who knows about it?”
Moderator“Is the community behaving well, and what needs my attention right now?”
Admin“What do I need to know, and what do I need to act on, across all the clubs?”

The decision: one product, with surfaces that respond to the role. The structure underneath is shared. What comes forward, and which actions are available, depends on how you enter.

04Key Design Decisions

SIX DECISIONS THAT SHAPED THE SYSTEM.

Decision 01

A Real Social Layer

People don't join ERGs to register for events. They join because they want to belong. If the platform can't feel like a real community, nobody comes back.

So the core is social: a home feed with three views, posts with images and video, and Buddies, which starts at onboarding and stays on every screen.

The rule I held: nothing should feel like a performance for the employer. Every feature had to serve the person's wish to connect, not the organization's wish to report participation.

Decision 02

One Structure, Five Entry Points

Separate interfaces per role are easier to design. One product is harder, but easier to use. And people here don't have one fixed role: a Club Owner is also a regular member of other clubs. Separate interfaces would split their experience.

So navigation and the available screens adapt to the person's role. Same objects, different framing and permissions.

Decision 03

Gamification That Rewards Depth, Not Frequency

Gamification can build momentum, or it can create anxiety. A less active member shouldn't feel ranked as less committed.

The milestones and the quarterly leaderboard reward meaningful participation, not raw activity counts.

Decision 04

Moderation as Triage, Not Surveillance

Moderators review content, event feedback and images. For content, there are three actions: Approve, Warn and Delete. Warn sits between doing nothing and deleting, so the community can hold a standard without punishing first.

The moderation screens show the items that need a decision. They don't browse member profiles or history.

Decision 05

One Design System for Web and Mobile

The Hub runs as a web portal and as a mobile PWA. Both come from one design system, and every component had to earn its place at both sizes. That constraint made the system better.

Decision 06

Analytics for ERG Leaders

The brief asked for a way to measure and show the impact of ERGs. I designed the analytics dashboard in Power BI: executive insights, clubs, events, rewards, social metrics and participant insights, all starting from one home page.

06Learnings

BELONGING FIRST.

An enterprise community platform is a different problem from an enterprise tool or a community app. Enterprise tools fail by being too rigid. Community platforms fail by being too loose.

Almost every time, I argued for belonging first. You can add governance later, but you can't bring back a community that never formed.

THE TEST OF AN ENTERPRISE SOCIAL PRODUCT IS WHETHER PEOPLE RETURN BECAUSE THEY WANT TO, NOT BECAUSE THEY HAVE TO.
